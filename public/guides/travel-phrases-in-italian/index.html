Italian for Travel

Travel Phrases in Italian: Practical Italian for Travelers

You do not need to speak fluent Italian to make travel easier. Learning a small set of practical phrases before your trip can help with restaurants, transportation, hotels, shopping, directions, and those everyday moments when a simple Italian phrase makes an interaction feel more comfortable.

Whether you are visiting Italy or another Italian-speaking destination, start with phrases you are actually likely to use. These essential Italian travel phrases cover some of the most common situations travelers encounter.

TravelLingo lets you practice these phrases before your trip with pronunciation audio and interactive activities, so they feel more familiar when you need them.

Why learn Italian before you travel?

Even a small amount of Italian can make everyday travel interactions easier and more comfortable.

You do not need to hold a long conversation. A few well-practiced phrases can help you greet someone politely, ask a basic question, order food, understand directions, or explain what you need.

The goal is not fluency before your trip. It is familiarity.

If you already recognize the phrases you are most likely to hear and use, you are less likely to freeze when you need them.

Start with polite basics

Some of the most useful Italian for travel is also the simplest. Greetings and courtesy phrases can be used almost anywhere.

Ciao

Hello

Buongiorno.

Good morning.

Buonasera.

Good evening.

Per favore.

Please.

Grazie

Thank you

Grazie mille

Thank you very much

Mi scusi.

Excuse me.

Arrivederci.

Goodbye.

In Italy and many Italian-speaking places, greeting someone before immediately asking a question is an important part of a polite interaction. Starting with buongiorno or buonasera, then using per favore and grazie, can make everyday exchanges feel more natural.

Italian for restaurants and cafés

Restaurants and cafés are some of the most common places travelers use another language. You do not need to memorize an entire menu to handle a few basic situations.

Il menù, per favore.

The menu, please.

Vorrei dell’acqua.

I would like water.

Vorrei un caffè.

I would like coffee.

Un tavolo per due, per favore.

A table for two, please.

Il conto, per favore.

The check, please.

Grazie, era delizioso.

Thank you, it was delicious.

Travelers with dietary needs may also find these useful:

Sono vegetariano/a.

I am vegetarian.

Non posso mangiare questo.

I cannot eat this.

TravelLingo also teaches useful individual words including acqua for water, caffè for coffee, birra for beer, and vino for wine.

Vorrei...

I would like...

Vorrei... is a useful phrase pattern because it gives you a polite way to make a simple request. For example: Vorrei un caffè.

Finding your way

Getting turned around in an unfamiliar place happens easily. A few direction phrases can help you ask for information and understand basic instructions.

Dov’è...?

Where is...

Dov’è il bagno?

Where is the bathroom?

Dov’è l’hotel?

Where is the hotel?

Come ci arrivo?

How do I get there?

È lontano?

Is it far?

Mi sono perso.

I got lost.

Non so dove sono.

I don't know where I am.

It also helps to recognize the direction words someone may use when answering you.

Sinistra

Left

Destra

Right

Dritto

Straight ahead

Vicino

Near

Lontano

Far

Strada

Street

Learning both the questions you may need to ask and the basic direction words you may hear in response makes this vocabulary much more useful.

Getting around by taxi, bus, and train

Transportation is another situation where a small set of familiar phrases can go a long way.

Ho bisogno di un taxi.

I need a taxi.

Devo andare all’aeroporto.

I need to go to the airport.

Dov’è la stazione ferroviaria?

Where is the train station?

Dov’è la fermata dell’autobus?

Where is the bus stop?

Quanto costa la corsa?

How much is the fare?

A che ora parte?

What time does it leave?

A che ora arriva?

What time does it arrive?

Questo autobus va in centro?

Does this bus go downtown?

Si fermi qui, per favore.

Please stop here.

For tickets, TravelLingo also includes:

Solo andata

One way

Andata e ritorno

Roundtrip

Un biglietto per...

A ticket to...

Aeroporto

Airport

Treno

Train

Autobus

Bus

These phrases can be useful for trains, buses, airport transportation, and other parts of your trip.

At your hotel

Hotel check-in is another good situation to practice before you travel.

Ho una prenotazione.

I have a booking.

Posso fare il check-in?

Can I check in?

A che ora è il check-out?

What time is check-out?

Dov’è l’ingresso?

Where is the entrance?

Qual è il codice della porta?

What is the door code?

Dov’è la password del Wi-Fi?

Where is the Wi-Fi password?

Ho bisogno di più asciugamani.

I need more towels.

La chiave non funziona.

The key does not work.

These phrases are useful for both traditional hotels and rentals, especially when you need an entrance code, Wi-Fi password, towels, or help with your room key.

Shopping in Italian

Markets, shops, and souvenir stores are another place where basic Italian can be helpful.

Quanto costa questo?

How much does this cost?

È troppo caro.

That is too expensive.

Sto solo guardando, grazie.

I am just looking, thank you.

Vorrei comprare questo.

I would like to buy this.

Ce l’ha in un’altra taglia?

Do you have this in another size?

Ce l’ha in un altro colore?

Do you have this in another color?

Posso provarlo?

Can I try it on?

Dov’è il camerino?

Where is the fitting room?

When it is time to pay:

Posso pagare con carta?

Can I pay with a card?

Posso pagare in contanti?

Can I pay with cash?

Posso avere una ricevuta?

Can I have a receipt?

TravelLingo also teaches taglia for size and troppo caro for too expensive. Payment habits vary by destination, so it is still worth checking whether cash, cards, or a combination of both is most commonly used where you are going.

When you do not understand

Some of the most valuable phrases are not about knowing the answer — they are about keeping a conversation going when you are unsure what someone said.

Non capisco.

I don’t understand.

Parli lentamente, per favore.

Please speak slowly.

Parla inglese?

Do you speak English?

These phrases can be just as useful as memorizing more vocabulary. They give you a way to slow the conversation down, ask for clarification, and keep communicating even when your Italian runs out.

When you need help

Hopefully you will not need emergency phrases during your trip, but they are worth knowing.

Aiuto

Help

Ho bisogno di aiuto.

I need help.

Ho bisogno di un medico.

I need a doctor.

Chiami un’ambulanza.

Call an ambulance.

Chiami la polizia.

Call the police.

Non mi sento bene.

I do not feel well.

Mi fa male qui.

It hurts here.

È un’emergenza.

This is an emergency.

Dov’è la farmacia?

Where is the pharmacy?

Ho un’allergia a questo.

I am allergic to this.

Ho un’allergia alimentare.

I have a food allergy.

Ospedale

Hospital

Farmacia

Pharmacy

You do not need to memorize every emergency phrase perfectly. Becoming familiar with a few of them before your trip can still make them easier to find or recognize if you ever need them.

Practice Italian pronunciation before your trip

Learning what a phrase means is only part of preparing to use it.

Listening to Italian phrases repeatedly helps you become familiar with how they sound. Saying them aloud can also make them easier to recall later when you are standing at a hotel desk, restaurant, train station, or shop.

You do not need a perfect accent. The goal is to become comfortable enough with the sound and rhythm of the phrase that you can recognize it and say it when you need it.

Focus on the phrases that matter most for your own trip, listen to them several times, and practice speaking them aloud.

Build a phrase list around your own trip

You probably do not need every phrase in this guide equally.

Think about the situations you are actually likely to encounter.

If you are staying in a hotel, prioritize check-in, Wi-Fi, and room-related phrases.

If you plan to use trains, learn how to ask about the station, tickets, departure times, and arrival times.

If cafés and restaurants are a big part of your trip, focus on ordering, asking for the bill, and communicating dietary needs.

A smaller list of phrases you have practiced several times is often more useful than a much longer list you barely recognize.

Italian changes from place to place

Italian is useful across Italy and in other Italian-speaking places, but everyday language is not exactly the same everywhere.

Pronunciation, accent, vocabulary, and local speech can vary by region. Food words, greetings, and everyday expressions may sound different from one city or region to another.

That does not mean one version is universally correct and another is wrong. Language simply reflects the place where it is spoken.

Start with broadly useful Italian travel phrases, then pick up a few destination-specific words or expressions when they are especially relevant to your trip. Standard Italian remains a practical foundation for travelers.

A simple way to prepare

Reading a phrase once is different from being able to remember it when someone is standing in front of you.

Before your trip, focus on a smaller number of useful phrases and practice them repeatedly.

  1. Choose the situations you are most likely to encounter.
  2. Learn a few phrases from each.
  3. Listen to the pronunciation.
  4. Practice saying them aloud.
  5. Review them again shortly before your trip.
