German for Travel

Travel Phrases in German: Practical German for Travelers

You do not need to speak fluent German to make everyday travel situations easier. Learning a small set of practical phrases before your trip can help with restaurants, hotels, trains and buses, shopping, directions, and simple conversations.

Whether you are visiting Germany, Austria, Switzerland, or another German-speaking destination, start with phrases you are actually likely to use. These essential German travel phrases cover some of the most common situations travelers encounter.

TravelLingo lets you practice these phrases before your trip with pronunciation audio and interactive activities, so they feel more familiar when you need them.

Why learn German before you travel?

Even a small amount of German can make everyday travel interactions easier and help you feel more prepared.

You do not need to hold a long conversation. A few well-practiced phrases can help you greet someone politely, ask a basic question, order food, understand directions, or explain what you need.

Even when English is available, knowing a few German words and phrases can make practical interactions smoother and more comfortable.

German is useful beyond Germany, including destinations in Austria and parts of Switzerland. Vocabulary and pronunciation can vary by place, so treat these phrases as a practical starting point.

Start with polite basics

Some of the most useful German for travel is also the simplest. Greetings and courtesy phrases can be used almost anywhere.

Hallo

Hello

Guten Morgen.

Good morning.

Guten Abend.

Good evening.

Bitte

Please.

Danke

Thank you

Vielen Dank.

Thank you very much.

Entschuldigung.

Excuse me.

Auf Wiedersehen.

Goodbye.

Even if the rest of the conversation happens in English, starting with a greeting and using bitte and danke can make an interaction feel more comfortable.

German for restaurants and cafés

Restaurants and cafés are some of the most common places travelers use another language. You do not need to memorize an entire menu to handle a few basic situations.

Die Speisekarte, bitte.

The menu, please.

Ich hätte gern Wasser.

I would like water.

Ich hätte gern Kaffee.

I would like coffee.

Einen Tisch für zwei, bitte.

A table for two, please.

Die Rechnung, bitte.

The check, please.

Danke, es war sehr lecker.

Thank you, it was delicious.

Travelers with dietary needs may also find these useful:

Ich bin Vegetarier/in.

I am vegetarian.

Ich kann das nicht essen.

I cannot eat this.

TravelLingo also teaches useful individual words including Wasser for water, Kaffee for coffee, Bier for beer, and Wein for wine.

Ich hätte gern...

I would like...

Ich hätte gern... is a useful phrase pattern because it gives you a polite way to make a simple request. For example: Ich hätte gern Kaffee.

Finding your way

Getting turned around in an unfamiliar place happens easily. A few direction phrases can help you ask for information and understand basic instructions.

Wo ist...?

Where is...

Wo ist die Toilette?

Where is the bathroom?

Wo ist das Hotel?

Where is the hotel?

Wie komme ich dorthin?

How do I get there?

Ist es weit?

Is it far?

Ich habe mich verlaufen.

I got lost.

Ich weiß nicht, wo ich bin.

I don't know where I am.

It also helps to recognize the direction words someone may use when answering you.

Links

Left

Rechts

Right

Geradeaus

Straight ahead

In der Nähe

Near

Weit weg

Far

Straße

Street

Learning both the questions you may need to ask and the basic direction words you may hear in response makes this vocabulary much more useful.

Getting around by taxi, bus, and train

Transportation is another situation where a small set of familiar phrases can go a long way.

Ich brauche ein Taxi.

I need a taxi.

Ich muss zum Flughafen.

I need to go to the airport.

Wo ist der Bahnhof?

Where is the train station?

Wo ist die Bushaltestelle?

Where is the bus stop?

Wie viel kostet die Fahrt?

How much is the fare?

Um wie viel Uhr fährt er ab?

What time does it leave?

Um wie viel Uhr kommt er an?

What time does it arrive?

Fährt dieser Bus ins Stadtzentrum?

Does this bus go downtown?

Halten Sie bitte hier an.

Please stop here.

Flughafen

Airport

Bus

Bus

These phrases can be useful for trains, buses, airport transportation, and other parts of your trip.

At your hotel

Hotel check-in is another good situation to practice before you travel.

Ich habe eine Buchung.

I have a booking.

Kann ich einchecken?

Can I check in?

Wann ist der Check-out?

What time is check-out?

Wo ist der Eingang?

Where is the entrance?

Wie lautet der Türcode?

What is the door code?

Wo ist das WLAN-Passwort?

Where is the Wi-Fi password?

Ich brauche mehr Handtücher.

I need more towels.

Der Schlüssel funktioniert nicht.

The key does not work.

These phrases are useful for both traditional hotels and rentals, especially when you need an entrance code, Wi-Fi password, towels, or help with your room key.

Shopping in German

Markets, shops, and souvenir stores are another place where basic German can be helpful.

Wie viel kostet das?

How much does this cost?

Das ist zu teuer.

That is too expensive.

Ich schaue mich nur um, danke.

I am just looking, thank you.

Ich möchte das kaufen.

I would like to buy this.

Haben Sie das in einer anderen Größe?

Do you have this in another size?

Haben Sie das in einer anderen Farbe?

Do you have this in another color?

Kann ich das anprobieren?

Can I try it on?

Wo ist die Umkleidekabine?

Where is the fitting room?

When it is time to pay:

Kann ich mit Karte bezahlen?

Can I pay with a card?

Kann ich bar bezahlen?

Can I pay with cash?

Kann ich eine Quittung bekommen?

Can I have a receipt?

TravelLingo also teaches Größe for size and zu teuer for too expensive. Payment habits vary by destination, so it is still worth checking whether cash, cards, or a combination of both is most commonly used where you are going.

When you do not understand

Some of the most valuable phrases are not about knowing the answer — they are about keeping a conversation going when you are unsure what someone said.

Ich verstehe nicht.

I don’t understand.

Sprechen Sie bitte langsam.

Please speak slowly.

Sprechen Sie Englisch?

Do you speak English?

These phrases can be just as useful as memorizing more vocabulary. They give you a way to slow the conversation down, ask for clarification, and keep communicating even when your German runs out.

When you need help

Hopefully you will not need emergency phrases during your trip, but they are worth knowing.

Hilfe

Help

Ich brauche Hilfe.

I need help.

Ich brauche einen Arzt.

I need a doctor.

Rufen Sie einen Krankenwagen.

Call an ambulance.

Rufen Sie die Polizei.

Call the police.

Ich fühle mich nicht wohl.

I do not feel well.

Es tut hier weh.

It hurts here.

Das ist ein Notfall.

This is an emergency.

Wo ist die Apotheke?

Where is the pharmacy?

Ich bin dagegen allergisch.

I am allergic to this.

Ich habe eine Lebensmittelallergie.

I have a food allergy.

Krankenhaus

Hospital

Apotheke

Pharmacy

You do not need to memorize every emergency phrase perfectly. Becoming familiar with a few of them before your trip can still make them easier to find or recognize if you ever need them.

Practice German pronunciation before your trip

German spelling and pronunciation may initially feel unfamiliar, so hearing phrases and practicing them aloud before travel can make them easier to recall.

Listening to German phrases repeatedly helps you become familiar with how they sound. Saying them aloud can also make them easier to remember later when you are standing at a hotel desk, restaurant, train station, or shop.

You do not need a perfect accent. The goal is to become comfortable enough with the sound and rhythm of the phrase that you can recognize it and say it when you need it.

Focus on the phrases that matter most for your own trip, listen to them several times, and practice speaking them aloud.

Build a phrase list around your own trip

You probably do not need every phrase in this guide equally.

Think about the situations you are actually likely to encounter.

If restaurants and cafés are a big part of your trip, focus on ordering, asking for the bill, and communicating dietary needs.

If you plan to use trains, learn how to ask about the station, tickets, departure times, and arrival times.

If you are staying in a hotel, prioritize check-in, Wi-Fi, and room-related phrases.

A smaller list of phrases you have practiced several times is often more useful than a much longer list you barely recognize.

German changes from place to place

German is spoken across Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and other German-speaking places, but everyday language is not exactly the same everywhere.

Pronunciation, accent, and vocabulary can vary by region and country. Words and expressions that feel common in one place may sound less familiar in another.

That does not mean one version is universally correct and another is wrong. Language simply reflects the place where it is spoken.

Start with broadly useful German travel phrases, then pick up a few destination-specific words when they are especially relevant to your trip. Standard German remains useful for many common travel interactions.

A simple way to prepare

Reading a phrase once is different from being able to remember it when someone is standing in front of you.

Before your trip, focus on a smaller number of useful phrases and practice them repeatedly.

  1. Think about the situations you are most likely to encounter.
  2. Choose a few useful phrases for each.
  3. Listen to their pronunciation.
  4. Practice saying them aloud.
  5. Review them again shortly before your trip.
