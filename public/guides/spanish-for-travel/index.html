Spanish for Travel

Spanish for Travel: Essential Phrases to Know Before Your Trip

You do not need to speak fluent Spanish to make travel easier. Learning a small set of practical phrases before your trip can help with greetings, restaurants, transportation, shopping, directions, hotels, and those moments when you simply need to ask for help.

Why learn Spanish before you travel?

Even a few useful Spanish phrases can make everyday travel interactions easier. Being able to greet someone, ask where something is, order a drink, or explain that you do not understand can help you feel more prepared when you arrive.

Spanish is useful across many destinations, not only Spain. Travelers encounter Spanish throughout much of Latin America, the Caribbean, and other Spanish-speaking communities around the world.

Vocabulary, pronunciation, and common expressions can vary from one destination to another, so think of these phrases as a practical starting point. Once you know the basics, you can also learn a few words or expressions that are especially useful for the place you are visiting.

Start with polite basics

Some of the most useful Spanish for travel is also the simplest. Greetings and courtesy phrases can be used almost anywhere.

Hola

Hello

Buenos días.

Good morning.

Buenas noches.

Good evening.

Por favor.

Please.

Gracias.

Thank you.

Muchas gracias.

Thank you very much.

Perdón.

Excuse me.

Lo siento.

Sorry.

Even if the rest of the conversation happens in English, starting with a greeting or using por favor and gracias can make an interaction feel more comfortable.

Spanish for restaurants and cafés

Restaurants and cafés are some of the most common places travelers use another language. You do not need to memorize an entire menu to handle a few basic situations.

El menú, por favor.

The menu, please.

Quisiera agua.

I would like water.

Quisiera café.

I would like coffee.

Una mesa para dos, por favor.

A table for two, please.

La cuenta, por favor.

The check, please.

Gracias, estuvo delicioso.

Thank you, it was delicious.

Travelers with dietary needs may also find these useful:

Soy vegetariano/a.

I am vegetarian.

No puedo comer esto.

I cannot eat this.

If you have allergies or specific food restrictions, learn the exact words you are likely to need before your trip rather than relying on only one general phrase.

TravelLingo also includes phrases such as:

Tengo una alergia alimentaria.

I have a food allergy.

Tengo alergia a esto.

I am allergic to this.

Finding your way

Getting turned around in an unfamiliar place happens easily. A few direction phrases can help you ask for information and understand basic instructions.

¿Dónde está...?

Where is...?

¿Dónde está el baño?

Where is the bathroom?

¿Dónde está el hotel?

Where is the hotel?

¿Cómo llego allí?

How do I get there?

¿Está lejos?

Is it far?

If you need to understand directions, these basic words are useful too:

Izquierda

Left

Derecha

Right

Derecho

Straight ahead

Cerca

Near

Lejos

Far

And if things do not go according to plan:

Me perdí.

I got lost.

No sé dónde estoy.

I don't know where I am.

Getting around by taxi, bus, and train

Transportation is another situation where a small set of familiar phrases can go a long way.

Necesito un taxi.

I need a taxi.

Necesito ir al aeropuerto.

I need to go to the airport.

¿Dónde está la estación de tren?

Where is the train station?

¿Dónde está la parada de autobús?

Where is the bus stop?

¿Cuánto cuesta el pasaje?

How much is the fare?

¿A qué hora sale?

What time does it leave?

¿A qué hora llega?

What time does it arrive?

¿Este autobús va al centro?

Does this bus go downtown?

For tickets, TravelLingo also includes:

Solo ida

One way

Ida y vuelta

Roundtrip

Un boleto a...

A ticket to...

These phrases can be useful for trains, buses, airport transportation, and other parts of your trip.

At your hotel

Hotel check-in is another good situation to practice before you travel.

Tengo una reserva.

I have a booking.

¿Puedo registrarme?

Can I check in?

¿A qué hora es la salida?

What time is check-out?

¿Dónde está la entrada?

Where is the entrance?

¿Dónde está la contraseña del Wi-Fi?

Where is the Wi-Fi password?

If something goes wrong, you may also need:

La llave no funciona.

The key does not work.

Necesito más toallas.

I need more towels.

¿Cuál es el código de la puerta?

What is the door code?

Learning a few accommodation-specific phrases before arrival can be especially useful when you are tired after a long travel day.

Shopping in Spanish

Markets, shops, and souvenir stores are another place where basic Spanish can be helpful.

¿Cuánto cuesta esto?

How much does this cost?

Demasiado caro

Too expensive

Solo estoy mirando, gracias.

I am just looking, thank you.

Me gustaría comprar esto.

I would like to buy this.

¿Tiene esto en otra talla?

Do you have this in another size?

¿Tiene esto en otro color?

Do you have this in another color?

¿Me lo puedo probar?

Can I try it on?

When it is time to pay:

¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?

Can I pay with a card?

¿Puedo pagar en efectivo?

Can I pay with cash?

¿Me puede dar un recibo?

Can I have a receipt?

Payment habits vary by destination, so it is still worth checking whether cash, cards, or a combination of both is most commonly used where you are going.

When you do not understand

Some of the most valuable phrases are not about knowing the answer — they are about keeping a conversation going when you are unsure what someone said.

No entiendo.

I don't understand.

Hable despacio, por favor.

Please speak slowly.

¿Habla inglés?

Do you speak English?

¿Puede repetirlo, por favor?

Can you repeat that?

¿Puede escribirlo, por favor?

Could you write that down, please?

¿Qué significa eso?

What does that mean?

These phrases can be just as useful as memorizing more vocabulary. They give you a way to slow the conversation down, ask for clarification, and keep communicating.

When you need help

Hopefully you will not need emergency phrases during your trip, but they are worth knowing.

Ayuda

Help

Necesito ayuda.

I need help.

Necesito un médico.

I need a doctor.

Llame a una ambulancia.

Call an ambulance.

Llame a la policía.

Call the police.

No me siento bien.

I do not feel well.

Me duele aquí.

It hurts here.

Es una emergencia.

This is an emergency.

¿Dónde está la farmacia?

Where is the pharmacy?

You do not need to memorize every emergency phrase perfectly. Becoming familiar with a few of them before your trip can still make them easier to find or recognize if you ever need them.

Practice Spanish pronunciation before your trip

Learning what a phrase means is only part of preparing to use it.

Listening to Spanish phrases repeatedly helps you become familiar with how they sound. Saying them aloud can also make them easier to recall later when you are standing at a hotel desk, restaurant, train station, or shop.

Focus on being understandable rather than trying to sound perfect. Spanish pronunciation and accents vary across regions, and practical communication matters more than perfect imitation.

Build a phrase list around your own trip

You probably do not need every phrase for every trip.

Instead, think about what you are actually planning to do.

If you are flying into a city, staying at a hotel, taking trains, eating at restaurants, and shopping, focus first on phrases from those situations.

A traveler staying at an all-inclusive resort may need a different set of Spanish phrases than someone traveling independently by train through several cities.

Useful categories to review include:

  • greetings and basics
  • directions
  • food and dining
  • hotels and accommodation
  • shopping
  • transportation
  • emergencies and health
  • numbers and money

A smaller set of phrases that you have heard and practiced several times is often more useful than a much larger list you barely remember.

Spanish changes from place to place

Spanish is spoken across many countries, and everyday language is not exactly the same everywhere.

Vocabulary, pronunciation, and expressions can vary between Spain, Mexico, Puerto Rico, Costa Rica, Colombia, Argentina, and other Spanish-speaking destinations.

That does not mean one version is universally correct and another is wrong. Language simply reflects the place where it is spoken.

Start with broadly useful Spanish travel phrases, then learn a few destination-specific words or expressions when they are especially relevant to your trip.

A simple way to prepare

You do not need a complicated study plan.

  1. Think about the situations you are likely to encounter.
  2. Choose a few useful phrases for each situation.
  3. Listen to their pronunciation.
  4. Say the phrases aloud.
  5. Review them again shortly before departure.
  6. Keep useful travel language available as a reference during your trip.
